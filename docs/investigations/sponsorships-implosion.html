Three world-class clubs, three breach notices: BlockDAG's sponsorship implosion

Borussia Dortmund. Inter Milan. Alpine Racing F1. Within twelve months, all three issued formal notices over unpaid BlockDAG sponsorship fees. The total exposure exceeds $4M and the pattern is identical: signing announcement, missed invoice, public default, marketing silence.

BlockDAG Exposed · Investigations Desk· Published 3 May 2026· 7 min read· Evidence cited →

In the eighteen months between the Borussia Dortmund signing and the Alpine Racing default, BlockDAG announced fifteen separate sports partnerships. Three of the largest — the only three big enough that we can reconstruct the financial terms from public records — all ended the same way.

The pattern starts with a flagship announcement. Hospitality video, hashtag, billboard. Then an invoice goes unpaid. Then a polite reminder. Then a formal notice. Then, eventually, public confirmation by the club. By the time the public hears of it, the marketing team has usually moved on to a new partnership.

Borussia Dortmund — the €2,007,444 default

BlockDAG signed a multi-year sleeve sponsorship with Bundesliga side Borussia Dortmund in September 2024 for €2,007,444 per season. The first season's invoice schedule was split into quarterly payments. Per BVB's public default notice, none of them were paid in full.

"BVB Borussia Dortmund GmbH & Co. KGaA hereby gives notice of default in the amount of €2,007,444.00 against BlockDAG Network Ltd…"— BVB legal counsel · Sep 1, 2025

Inter Milan — cease & desist

The Inter Milan deal was smaller and structured differently — a regional sleeve and digital placement package. After invoices for Q1 and Q2 2025 went unpaid, Inter filed for material breach in June. By August they had served a cease & desist demanding all use of club imagery stop immediately.

Some BlockDAG marketing pages still featured the Nerazzurri crest weeks after the C&D was served.

Alpine Racing F1 — the December that broke the public CEO

Alpine was the most public default and the one that triggered the management shake-up. Announced with great fanfare in September 2025 — livery, trackside, the lot — for a single-season $1.4M fee. The first invoice came due in October. By the December deadline, no payment had landed.

Within ten days, Antony Turner was no longer CEO and Gurhan Kiziloz had been publicly named as the project's actual founder for the first time.

The bigger picture

Three signings, three defaults — and the same December that the Alpine deadline lapsed, BlockDAG's ~24 in-house employees stopped getting paid. The same month, on-chain analysts traced ~$110M in stablecoins routed to Middle East OTC desks. Read that trace →
