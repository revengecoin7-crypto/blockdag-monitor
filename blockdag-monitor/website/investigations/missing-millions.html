$442 million claimed, less than $100 million on-chain — the BlockDAG presale's missing money.

Over 450 days, BlockDAG Network publicly raised "$442 million." Its CEO later said the figure was closer to $200 million. Blockchain investigator ZachXBT found less than $100 million ever moved on-chain. At an investor meeting on January 6, 2026, leadership disclosed only $32 million had been earmarked for post-launch liquidity. Where did the rest go?

BlockDAG Exposed · Investigations Desk · Published 4 May 2026 · 14 min read · 12 primary sources cited →

For two years and one month, BlockDAG Network told its community it had raised $442 million. The figure appeared in marketing emails. It appeared in podcast interviews. It appeared on the homepage, in pinned Telegram posts, and in every press release announcing the next sponsorship. Then on a private call in January 2026, the new CEO said something else.

The audio leaked within 48 hours. On the recording, Nic Van Den Bergh — the former Chief Marketing Officer who had been promoted to CEO three weeks earlier, after his predecessor Antony Turner was fired — walked back the headline figure in a single sentence. The $442 million was, he said, "always more aspirational." The real number was "closer to $200 million." Then he disclosed the figure that mattered for anyone planning to sell BDAG on launch day: roughly $32 million had been earmarked for post-launch liquidity.

By every standard the project itself had set, those three numbers cannot all be true.

The headline raise that wasn't

BlockDAG's presale opened in April 2024 at $0.001 per BDAG and ran for 450 days. The pitch was simple and constant: BDAG would launch at $0.05 — a 50× return for early entrants and a $1 billion day-one market cap. The marketing budget reflected that confidence. By mid-2025 the project was sponsoring Borussia Dortmund (€2,007,444 a season), Inter Milan, and Alpine Racing F1, and renting Times Square billboards.

To pay for that, the headline raise figure ticked steadily upward. $50 million, then $100 million, then $200 million, then $300 million, then $442 million. The figure was never accompanied by a wallet address. It was never accompanied by an auditor's name. It was, in the words of the BlockDAG help desk to a community member who asked, "verified internally."

"BDAG will launch at $0.05. There is no scenario where this changes. We have the liquidity, we have the listings."— Telegram · Official Channel · Apr 12, 2025

ZachXBT looks at the chain

In October 2025, the investigative outlet DL News published a piece questioning whether the figure was real. The on-chain work was contributed by the pseudonymous blockchain investigator ZachXBT. His method was the standard one: identify deposit addresses controlled by the project, sum incoming stablecoin transfers, and compare to the claim.

The total he found, across 2,847 distinct deposit addresses and every public stablecoin, was $94,612,883. Less than a quarter of the public number. Less than half of the CEO's later walk-back. He published the wallet list. Anyone with a block explorer can re-run the count.

The $32 million disclosure

Three months after the DL News story, a small group of larger investors were invited to a private call. The recording — about 47 minutes long — covers product roadmap, sponsorship status, and "expectations for launch." The relevant disclosure comes about 22 minutes in.

"We're looking at about $32 million for liquidity at launch… the $442 figure was always more aspirational; closer to $200."— Nic Van Den Bergh, CEO · Jan 6, 2026 · leaked audio

$32 million is the number that matters because it is the only one that has to actually exist. To list a token at $0.05 with the marketed $1 billion market cap, you need committed liquidity proportional to expected day-one volume. $32 million across three small CEXs and no DEX route is not that. It is, instead, the number that explains what happened on March 4, 2026.

What launch day actually looked like

BDAG mainnet went live as scheduled. The opening trade printed at $0.000503 — a 99% discount to the promised launch price. Day-one listings appeared on three small exchanges; no Tier-1 venue, no DEX route. Within 72 hours BDAG was trading below $0.0001, where it remains. See the full price-vs-promise table →

The whistleblower's claim

On March 14, ten days after launch, BlockDAG's former payroll manager Liza Van Den Berg posted a public statement. She had been let go in December along with the rest of the finance team and was, she said, owed three months of salary. Her core allegation was not about money owed to her.

"I was the payroll manager. I will say it plainly: the presale was oversold by over 100%. There was not enough BDAG reserved for what we sold."— Liza Van Den Berg · Mar 14, 2026

If accurate, this compounds the on-chain shortfall in a specific way. ZachXBT's analysis answers the question "did the money come in?" Van Den Berg's claim answers a different one: "was the supply ever there to deliver?" A presale oversold by 100% would mean the project had committed to delivering twice as many tokens as it had reserved for the round.

BlockDAG has not responded to her allegation. Her sworn declaration, she has said publicly, is being prepared.

The $110 million trail to OTC desks

In April, a follow-up on-chain analysis traced ~$110 million in stablecoins routed through Middle East OTC desks and into deposit accounts at Binance and BTSE in the months immediately preceding launch. The destination accounts are not disclosed in any BlockDAG report or financial communication.

Three things are worth noting. The total ($110M) exceeds ZachXBT's earlier on-chain total ($94M) — the difference is timing; the OTC analysis covers a wider window and counts hops. The destination is exchanges that are, by design, harder to trace through. And the timing — concentrated in November and December 2025 — overlaps exactly with the period when employees stopped getting paid.

Where this leaves the math

Three numbers were given for a single fact, the size of the BlockDAG raise:

  • $442 million — the public, marketed figure, repeated for 24 months across every channel.
  • ~$200 million — the CEO's January 2026 walk-back, on a private call.
  • <$100 million — the on-chain total in BlockDAG-controlled addresses, per ZachXBT.

And one number was given for what was actually available to support a market on launch day: $32 million. Whichever of the three raise figures is closest to true, the gap between it and the $32 million is large, the destination of that gap is not disclosed, and no auditor has ever been named to verify any of it.

BlockDAG was given an opportunity to comment for this story. They did not respond. We will update the page if they do.
